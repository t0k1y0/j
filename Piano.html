About this piece

Play from your computer keyboard. A S D F G H J K are the white keys from C, and W E T Y U the black keys between them. Z and X move an octave down or up. Space plays and pauses.

Reading the score

The grand staff

Two five-line staves joined together. The upper one has the treble (G) clef, whose curl circles the G above middle C; the right hand usually reads it. The lower has the bass (F) clef, whose two dots sit either side of the F below middle C; that is the left hand's.

Middle C

The C nearest the centre of the piano (marked with a red dot on the keys here). It sits on a short ledger line just below the treble staff, or just above the bass staff. Find it as the white key left of the pair of black keys in the middle.

How long a note lasts

Counted in beats, where a quarter note is one beat. A dot after a note adds half its value again. Rests are silences of the same lengths.

Time signature

The two numbers at the start. The top says how many beats are in each bar; the bottom which note gets one beat (4 a quarter, 8 an eighth). So 3/4 is three quarter-note beats to the bar, the waltz and minuet metre.

Sharps, flats and naturals

A sharp (♯) raises a note to the very next key on the right, usually a black one; a flat (♭) lowers it to the next on the left; a natural (♮) cancels either. Written beside a note, it lasts to the end of the bar. Written at the start of each line (the key signature), it applies to that note everywhere.

How to practise

Learn each hand alone first, slowly: set the tempo low and use Wait for me, so the music moves only when you are right. Curve your fingers, keep your wrists level, and number the fingers 1 (thumb) to 5 (little finger) on both hands. Speed up only once it is easy.
