    Writing a melody

    • Start from the chords. Load a progression or build one; the melody sits on top of it.
    • Put chord tones (the tinted cells) on beats 1 and 3. Other notes can pass between them.
    • Move mostly by step. After a leap, step back the other way to balance it.
    • Repeat a short idea. Let bar 3 echo bar 1 and change the ending, like a question and answer.
    • Give the tune one high point, near the middle or the end.
    • Finish on Do, the key note, over the I chord, and hold it.

    Singing basics

    • Stand tall with shoulders relaxed and knees soft; keep your jaw loose.
    • Breathe low: let your belly and lower ribs expand rather than lifting your shoulders.
    • Start each note gently, without a push of air or a click in the throat.
    • Learn the pitch first by listening, then sing it; Echo mode is built for that.
    • Work in the middle of your range before stretching it, a half step at a time.
    • Stop if singing hurts or you get hoarse. Sip water and rest your voice.

    Practice melodies

    Work left to right: hear the key, tap the rhythm, then sing. With the microphone on, each note turns green or red after you sing. Headphones keep the click out of the microphone. Click any note to hear it.

    Rhythm values

    Counting in 4/4. “Say” uses the rhythm syllables common in choir and classroom teaching. Hear plays one bar with a click on each beat.

    A daily practice plan

    • Ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week.
    • Warm up with the solfège ladder on the Sing tab (2 minutes).
    • Sing two or three melodies at your level. Before each one: find Do, hear the key, tap the rhythm, scan for patterns.
    • Sing on solfège first, then on “la” to test whether you really hear the notes.
    • Keep the beat going. If you get lost, rejoin at the next bar line.
    • Use “Piano sings with me” only to learn a melody; turn it off to test yourself.
    • Move up a level after three clean melodies in a row, and drop back if a level feels like guessing.
